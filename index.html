Dataset Politics

I came to AI by accident. When image generation platforms first opened to the public, I started exploring what they could make and almost immediately ran into what they refused to make. Prompts using words like queer or gay were flagged. My account was warned. These platforms had already decided who they were not built for.

That moment sent me down a path I have not left. I began studying how these platforms represent and erase people like me. I discovered that queerness was not absent from the dataset by accident. It had been systematically filtered, coded as unsafe, removed as a category of human experience worthy of being pictured. The AI did not invent this erasure. It inherited it from the society that built it.

My practice lives in that gap. I work across a variety of digital mediums, primarily AI image generation and video, to explore the past, present and future of queer temporality and identity. I use platforms that utilize public datasets, which means the bias I am interrogating is baked into the very tools I am using to interrogate it.

And I have begun documenting the refusals those systems produce. The error message. The account warning. The sanitized workaround prompt sitting next to the original that the platform blocked. These are not footnotes to my work. They are the work.

I am making this for the future. For the queer people who come after us, so they can look back and know we were here, that we tried, that we refused to be erased from the record even when the record was built to erase us.
